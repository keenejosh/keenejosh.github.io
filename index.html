Doctoral & Master's Work

Research

Doctoral Research · PAMP-Induced Tau Pathology & Neuroinflammation

I examined whether repeated challenges with diverse peripheral immune stimuli drive tau pathology and neuroinflammation in PS19 tauopathy mice, with tissue collected 30 days after the final challenge to assess longer-term consequences of immune activation.

Primary Finding

  • PAMP administration increased hippocampal p-tau (AT8) 2.8-fold and whole-hemisphere p-tau 3.0-fold, while total tau (HT7) remained unchanged.

Supporting Finding

  • A synergistic genotype × treatment interaction for MHC II and positive correlations between p-tau and glial reactivity markers support a relationship between peripheral immune activation, tau pathology, and neuroinflammation.
Dissertation (PDF)

Master's Thesis · Effects of Prenatal Inflammatory Stress on Amyloid Deposition & Microglial Density

I examined whether prenatal inflammatory stress alters amyloid plaque deposition and microglial density in 5xFAD offspring, comparing mice born to 5xFAD versus Non-Tg dams and assessing sex differences in these effects.

Primary Finding

  • Prenatal inflammatory exposure paradoxically reduced hippocampal amyloid plaque burden (6E10) by 46% in male and 59% in female offspring of 5xFAD dams compared with offspring of Non-Tg dams.

Supporting Finding

  • Microglial density was unchanged despite reduced amyloid plaque burden, suggesting that prenatal inflammatory stress may alter amyloid pathology independently of changes in microglial quantity.
